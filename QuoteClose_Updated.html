The Ghost Effect

Once they have your quote, they have what they need.

Quoting takes real work: drawings, materials, machine time, pricing. Then the quote goes out, and the buyer goes quiet. They've got your number, and you've lost your leverage.

A thoughtful follow-up conversation can uncover timing, pricing concerns, or a project that has changed direction. Without it, too many quotes simply go quiet.

When you send a quote, what happens next?

Who follows up, when, and what do they say? If there's no set process, the answer is usually nobody.

How do you know follow-up is actually happening?

When the shop gets busy, customer follow-up is often the first thing to drop. An experienced sales professional keeps the conversation moving.

Are you only hearing back on the wins?

If lost quotes just go quiet, you never learn whether it was price, timing, or something you could have fixed.
